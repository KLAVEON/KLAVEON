(02)Project Challenge

Owning the space between clinical and fluffy.

Beauty and wellness is crowded. Many brands claim glow, ingestibles and supplements, and few combine drinkable, beauty-first and community-driven in one distinct voice that works in Nigeria and abroad.

SKIN by Tea was entering with no awareness, no heritage and no audience. The category sounds either clinical and cold or fluffy and vague, and the brand needed a clear identity early so it would not become another wellness tea.

(THE PROBLEMS)
  • Category: drinkable wellness feels like supplements, and beauty feels external
  • Positioning: the playfulness of Poppi and Glossier, with the credibility of Fenty Skin and Rare Beauty
  • Market: launch in Lagos, built for Dubai, the UK and the US from day one
(03)Insight

The sip is the moment.

Most beauty brands sell outcomes and tell the audience what they will look like afterwards. In this category the act of drinking is the experience, so the ritual is the product.

Moments are shareable where routines are not. That gave the brand a community mechanic: a behaviour the audience can perform and post.

(04)Brand Strategy

Beauty in the Moment.

Every sip is a ritual moment that ripples outward: Pause, Sip, Radiate. The line disciplines every creative decision inside the brand and gives the audience something to do outside it.

(05)Visual Identity

Design as evidence of the idea.

A water drop sits at the N of SKIN, the moment of infusion. A capsule around "by Tea" reads as a wellness product and as a lip mid-sip. Four named colours and two gradients carry the product lines.

(WHAT WE DID)
  • Logo: horizontal and stacked lockups, monochrome versions, logomark, favicon, co-branding lockup, prohibited use
  • Colour: Mint Pop #99E4D2, Lilac Pop #CA9FE2, Sun Glow #FFD74D, Coral Pop #FF6F61, off-white #F4F4EF, black and two gradients
  • Typography: Catchye Demo and Haas Grotesk Display, with a typographic hierarchy
  • Photography direction
(06)Verbal Identity and Packaging

A brand that speaks before it is opened.

Pause. Sip. Radiate. works as a tagline and as an instruction. Secondary lines such as "Drinkable Skincare" and "What's Your Ritual Today?" follow the same logic: they describe an action, and the audience stands inside the moment.

On the gift box the line becomes "Open. Sip. Radiate.", so the first contact with the brand is an instruction.

(WHAT WE DID)
  • Verbal system and owned language
  • Vial and gift box packaging
  • Website and social media applications
  • Stationery: business card and event tag
(07)KLAVEON Reflection

The idea came first, and the logo followed.

The Drinkable Brand Guide traces every page back to one line: Beauty in the Moment. The strategy sets the line and the system carries it, from the four colours and two gradients to the two typefaces.

The guide also sets limits. A prohibited-use page and a co-branding lockup keep the mark intact when partners, printers and event teams handle it. The test KLAVEON applies to every system is whether someone could recognise the brand without seeing the logo.
